Dutch Oven Pork Carnitas

Prep20 min
Cook4 hr 20 min
Total4 hr 40 min
Serves18
SkillMedium
Scale

Classic braised-then-fried carnitas, built for a full bone-in pork shoulder in a Dutch oven. Orange and lime keep the braising liquid bright while four hours in a low oven does the real work; a two-stage broil crisps the edges without drying out the middle. An optional stovetop pan-reduce finish gets you there too, if you'd rather skip the broiler. Built for taco night with a crowd, with plenty left over to freeze.

      Notes
      • Adding the bone to the braise gives the liquid noticeably more body — worth doing every time. Pick any extra meat off it before discarding.
      • Optional finishing method: strain and pan-reduce (in place of steps 5–7). After the 4-hour braise, remove the bone, bay leaves, orange halves, and onion and discard — leave the garlic in. Return the meat, still in whole chunks (don't shred yet — it's fine if it's fallen apart a little), to the pot with the remaining liquid. Simmer uncovered over medium-high heat, stirring occasionally, until the liquid cooks off and the chunks start frying and browning in their own rendered fat, about 15–25 minutes depending on how much liquid is left. Once well browned on the cut edges, remove from heat, pull the chunks apart into shreds right in the pot, and serve.
      • If using the pan-reduce method, keep the meat in chunks (not shredded) until after browning — shredded meat has too much surface area and turns dry and stringy over that much reduction time.
      • Garlic cloves are fine left in through either finishing method — they soften further and add flavor; some may melt into the fat.
      • The onion should come out before either finishing step — left in that long, it turns slimy and stringy.
      • If your Dutch oven can't fit all 9 lbs comfortably, cut chunks a bit smaller rather than crowding — you want braising liquid touching most of the meat.
      • Reserve a little extra fat and liquid before finishing either way, in case the meat needs moistening again at serving time. It also freezes well for the next batch.
      To Serve
      • Double up corn tortillas — two per taco. Corn tears more easily than flour, so stacking gives structural integrity.
      • Warm tortillas properly — a few seconds directly over a gas flame or in a dry hot skillet until they blister slightly. This is what makes corn tortillas taste right, not just soft.
      • Diced white onion and cilantro — the classic simple topping, lets the carnitas be the star.
      • Salsa verde or salsa roja — verde (tomatillo) cuts through the richness of the fat nicely.
      • Lime wedges on the side.
      • Pickled red onion for acidity and crunch beyond raw onion — quick-pickle sliced red onion in lime juice and salt while the pork braises.
      • Crumbled queso fresco or cotija if you want a little more richness and saltiness.
      • Diced pineapple is optional but great with the citrus-braised flavor — leans a bit more al pastor.
      • Beyond straight tacos: the same fillings work well piled into a torta, scattered over crispy tostadas with refried beans underneath, or as nachos.
